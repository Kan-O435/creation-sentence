# SEO topic design

Create SEO candidates at packaging time so one published article becomes a coherent topic cluster. Validate them in the next issue's research stage before writing.

## Create a candidate

For each candidate, write one natural Japanese primary query, not a bag of keywords. Identify the intent: `know` (understand), `do` (take an action), `compare` (choose), or `buy` (evaluate a paid solution). Then define a reader problem and a promise that differs from all existing articles.

Use this template:

```markdown
### <working title>
- primary_query: <one query>
- intent: know | do | compare | buy
- reader_problem: <specific stuck point>
- promise: <outcome without an unsupported guarantee>
- format: checklist | how-to | comparison | experience | glossary
- internal_link: <source article / planned destination>
- evidence_status: hypothesis | validated
- validation_next: <SERP / note search / reader-question check>
```

## Quality rules

- Give each article a distinct intent and promise; do not split one thin answer into many near-duplicates.
- Do not promise ranking, traffic, revenue, or a relationship outcome.
- Use the exact query naturally in the title, opening, one heading, and metadata only when it remains readable. Never keyword-stuff.
- Prefer a useful answer over a broad keyword. Add first-hand evidence, cited sources, a tool, a checklist, or a worked example.
- Record the date and source when validating demand, competition, or platform search behaviour. Until then, label it `hypothesis`.
- Link only to genuinely relevant published or planned articles; never invent an internal URL.
